Expected Result

A meaningful <img> has a text alternative that conveys the same information as the image.

Actual Result

Assistive technology users do not get the information the image conveys.

Solution

Add an alt attribute to the <img> that concisely conveys the image's information.

Failing Example

No text alternative

This image has no text alternative.

How a screen reader reads it:

  • NVDA: "graphic"

<img src="mars-rover.png">

title and alt=""

The title attribute is treated as a fallback, so if an empty alt attribute exists, this takes priority over the title text, and the image is treated as decorative.

How a screen reader reads it:

  • NVDA: no announcement

<img 
    src="mars-rover.png" 
    alt="" title="An artist's concept of a NASA Mars Exploration Rover on the surface of Mars.">

Passing Example

alt text

Using the native HTML alt attribute is the preferred method for providing a text alternative for an <img>.

How a screen reader reads it:

  • NVDA: "graphic, An artist's concept of a NASA Mars Exploration Rover on the surface of Mars. "

<img 
    src="mars-rover.png" 
    alt="An artist's concept of a NASA Mars Exploration Rover on the surface of Mars.">

aria-label

Using the aria-label attribute is also an acceptable method for providing a text alternative.

How a screen reader reads it:

  • NVDA: "graphic, An artist's concept of a NASA Mars Exploration Rover on the surface of Mars. "

<img 
    src="mars-rover.png" 
    aria-label="An artist's concept of a NASA Mars Exploration Rover on the surface of Mars.">

title

The title attribute provides a tooltip, and it is generally read by screen readers. However, while it may pass testing with certain screen readers, it is not guaranteed to work across all environments.

How a screen reader reads it:

  • NVDA: "graphic, An artist's concept of a NASA Mars Exploration Rover on the surface of Mars. "

<img 
    src="mars-rover.png" 
    title="An artist's concept of a NASA Mars Exploration Rover on the surface of Mars.">

Disabilities Affected

  • Blindness/Visual Impairment: People who cannot perceive the image need to have a text alternative so that the image can be interpreted by assistive technology such as screen readers and braille devices.
  • Cognitive Impairment: People who have difficulty understanding images may benefit from having a text description.
