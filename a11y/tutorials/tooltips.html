Tooltips

A tooltip is a short, non-interactive popup that provides contextual information about an element. A tooltip typically appears when a user hovers over an element with a pointer or moves keyboard focus to the element.

Tooltips differ from other popups:

  • A tooltip contains brief, non-interactive descriptive content and does not receive keyboard focus.
  • Use a dialog, popover, disclosure, or another appropriate pattern when the popup contains links, buttons, form controls, or other interactive content.
  • Use a visible label or instruction instead of a tooltip when information is necessary to understand or operate a control.
  • Do not use a tooltip as the only accessible name for an icon-only control.
  • Use a tooltip to supplement an already understandable control, not to conceal essential instructions or error information.

WCAG requirements

  • The tooltip trigger has a programmatically determinable accessible name that identifies its purpose without relying on the tooltip. (2.4.6, 4.1.2)
  • If the tooltip contains information needed to understand the trigger, the tooltip content is programmatically associated with the trigger. (1.3.1, 4.1.2)
  • A tooltip that appears on pointer hover also appears when its trigger receives keyboard focus, when the information is needed to understand or operate the trigger. (2.1.1, 1.3.1)
  • The tooltip is displayed in a manner that does not prevent keyboard users from operating the trigger. (2.1.1)
  • Keyboard focus remains on the trigger while the tooltip is displayed. (2.4.3)
  • The tooltip does not contain focusable or interactive content. (2.1.1, 2.1.2, 4.1.2)
  • When additional content appears on hover or focus and obscures or replaces other content, users can dismiss it without moving pointer hover or keyboard focus. (1.4.13)
  • If pointer hover triggers the tooltip, users can move the pointer over the tooltip without causing it to disappear. (1.4.13)
  • The tooltip remains visible until the pointer hover or keyboard focus is removed, the user dismisses it, or the information is no longer valid. (1.4.13)
  • The tooltip does not obscure the trigger or other content needed to operate the page, unless users can dismiss it without moving pointer hover or keyboard focus. (1.4.13)
  • Tooltip text has sufficient contrast against its background. (1.4.3)
  • Tooltip borders, icons, and other meaningful non-text indicators have sufficient contrast. (1.4.11)
  • The tooltip remains readable and usable at 200% text resize, at a 320-CSS-pixel-wide viewport, and when text-spacing overrides are applied. (1.4.4, 1.4.10, 1.4.12)
  • The tooltip does not cause an unexpected change of context merely because the trigger receives focus. (3.2.1)

Tooltips for labeled controls

A labeled control tooltip provides supplemental information about an already understandable button, link, form field, or other control.

  • The control remains understandable and operable without opening the tooltip. (2.4.6, 3.3.2, 4.1.2)
  • The tooltip provides supplementary information rather than the control's only accessible name. (4.1.2)
  • If the tooltip provides instructions necessary to complete a task, the instructions are available through a persistent or otherwise accessible method. (3.3.2)

Tooltips for icon-only controls

An icon-only control may display a tooltip that provides a visible description, but the control must have an accessible name independently of the tooltip.

  • The icon-only button or link has a programmatically determinable accessible name that describes its action or destination. (4.1.2)
  • The tooltip is not the only way to discover the purpose of the control. (2.4.6, 4.1.2)
  • The tooltip content is available to keyboard users as well as pointer users when it provides useful descriptive information. (2.1.1)
  • The icon has a text alternative, or is hidden from assistive technology when the parent control already has an appropriate accessible name. (1.1.1, 4.1.2)

Form-field help tooltips

A form-field help tooltip provides supplementary guidance about a field, such as formatting examples or an explanation of why information is requested.

  • The field has a visible label and an accessible name independent of the tooltip. (3.3.2, 4.1.2)
  • Instructions needed to enter a valid value are available to all users, including users who do not hover or focus the help trigger. (3.3.2)
  • If the help content is necessary to understand a required format, users can access it using the keyboard and assistive technology. (2.1.1, 3.3.2, 4.1.2)
  • Error messages are not provided only in a tooltip. Error information identifies the field and is available after validation. (3.3.1, 3.3.3)

Best practices

These practices improve usability and robustness, although not all are separate WCAG requirements in every implementation.

  • Use native HTML labels, visible text, instructions, and help text before relying on a tooltip.
  • Keep tooltip content brief and directly related to its trigger.
  • Use plain language and avoid duplicating the trigger's label without adding useful information.
  • Use a short delay before displaying a pointer-hover tooltip to avoid showing tooltips accidentally as users move the pointer across the page.
  • Do not use a tooltip for long instructions, legal text, error messages, or content users must read before continuing.
  • Do not put links, buttons, form fields, or other interactive controls inside a tooltip.
  • Do not make the tooltip itself keyboard focusable.
  • Use role="tooltip" on a custom tooltip container.
  • Use aria-describedby on the trigger to reference the tooltip when the tooltip supplies a description that should be available to assistive technology.
  • Do not use aria-label or aria-labelledby on the tooltip itself to replace the trigger's accessible name.
  • Use Escape as a conventional method for dismissing a tooltip when a dismissal method is needed.
  • Ensure that dismissing a tooltip does not move keyboard focus or change the trigger's state.
  • Position the tooltip close to its trigger without obscuring the trigger, adjacent controls, or the content it describes.
  • Ensure the tooltip stays within the viewport and can be read at high zoom.
  • Do not use hover-only tooltips for touch-only functionality. Provide the information by another available means.
  • When an icon-only element needs a name, provide an accessible name on the button or link itself; a tooltip may provide supplementary visible help.

Practical test sequence

Focus Details Applicable Criteria
Identify function
  • Confirm that the popup is a tooltip: it provides brief, non-interactive descriptive text and does not require users to take an action within it. (1.3.1, 4.1.2)
  • Determine what type of tooltip it is (e.g., labeled control, icon-only control, form field help).
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 1.3.1 Info and Relationships (Level A)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 4.1.2 Name, Role, Value (Level A)
Semantics and language
  • Confirm that the trigger has a meaningful accessible name that does not depend on the tooltip. (2.4.6, 4.1.2)
  • Confirm that essential instructions, labels, and error information are not supplied only through a tooltip. (3.3.1, 3.3.2)
  • For a custom tooltip, confirm that the tooltip container has role="tooltip". (4.1.2)
  • Confirm that the trigger references the tooltip with aria-describedby when its content is intended to be available as the trigger's accessible description. (1.3.1, 4.1.2)
  • Confirm that the tooltip does not contain interactive or focusable content. (2.1.1, 2.1.2)

  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 2.4.6 Headings and Labels (Level AA)

  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 3.3.2 Labels or Instructions (Level A)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 4.1.2 Name, Role, Value (Level A)
Screen reader
  • Move focus to the trigger and confirm that its accessible name, role, and description are available. (4.1.2)
  • Confirm that tooltip content intended to supplement the trigger is announced or otherwise available through the trigger's accessible description. (1.3.1, 4.1.2)
  • Confirm that an icon-only trigger has a meaningful accessible name independent of the tooltip. (1.1.1, 4.1.2)
  • Confirm that decorative icons are not announced unnecessarily. (1.1.1)
  • Confirm that the tooltip itself does not become an unexpected focus stop. (2.1.1, 2.4.3)
  • Confirm that form instructions and error information remain available even when users do not open the tooltip. (3.3.1, 3.3.2)

  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 3.3.1 Error Identification (Level A)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 3.3.2 Labels or Instructions (Level A)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 4.1.2 Name, Role, Value (Level A)
Keyboard only
  • Use Tab and Shift+Tab to move to and away from the tooltip trigger. (2.1.1, 2.4.3)
  • Confirm that the tooltip appears when its trigger receives keyboard focus when the tooltip information is needed to understand or operate the trigger. (2.1.1, 3.3.2)
  • Confirm that keyboard focus remains on the trigger while the tooltip is displayed. (2.4.3)
  • Confirm that the tooltip does not create an additional keyboard-focus stop. (2.1.1, 2.4.3)
  • Confirm that the trigger has a visible focus indicator and is not completely hidden by the tooltip or other author-created content. (2.4.7, 2.4.11)
  • If the tooltip obscures or replaces other content, press Escape or use another available dismissal method and confirm that the tooltip closes without moving focus. (1.4.13)
  • Confirm that the tooltip does not cause a change of context merely because its trigger receives focus. (3.2.1)

  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 3.2.1 On Focus (Level A)
Pointer and touch
  • Move the pointer over the trigger and confirm that the tooltip appears when hover behavior is provided. (1.4.13)
  • Move the pointer from the trigger onto the tooltip and confirm that the tooltip remains visible long enough to read. (1.4.13)
  • Confirm that the tooltip does not disappear merely because the pointer moves from the trigger to the tooltip. (1.4.13)
  • If the tooltip obscures or replaces other content, confirm that users can dismiss it without moving the pointer. (1.4.13)
  • Confirm that the tooltip does not prevent pointer access to the trigger or other controls required to complete the task. (1.4.13)
  • On touch devices, confirm that tooltip-only information is available through another method when it is needed to understand or operate the trigger. (2.1.1, 3.3.2)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 1.4.13 Content on Hover or Focus (Level AA)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 2.1.1 Keyboard (Level A)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 3.3.2 Labels or Instructions (Level A)
Inspect visual aspects
  • Check tooltip-text contrast against the tooltip background. (1.4.3)
  • Check contrast for tooltip borders, close indicators, icons, and other meaningful non-text information. (1.4.11)
  • Confirm that the tooltip remains readable at 200% text resize, at a 320-CSS-pixel-wide viewport, and with text-spacing overrides. Text must not clip, overlap, or require unnecessary horizontal scrolling. (1.4.4, 1.4.10, 1.4.12)
  • Confirm that the tooltip remains within the viewport and does not conceal its trigger, focus indicator, or nearby controls needed to complete a task. (1.4.10, 1.4.13, 2.4.11)
  • Confirm that tooltip visibility is not communicated by color alone when color is used to identify the trigger's help state. (1.4.1)
