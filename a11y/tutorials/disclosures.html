Disclosures

A disclosure is a control that shows or hides a related section of content. Examples include a "Show more" control, an expandable card, a filter section, supplementary instructions, or a compact navigation menu.

A disclosure differs from an accordion:

  • A disclosure is a single show/hide component or an independently operated disclosure control.
  • An accordion is a vertically stacked set of related disclosure sections, usually with heading structure and accordion-specific navigation considerations.
  • Use a disclosure when the user needs to reveal or hide related content without navigating to another page or opening a dialog.
  • Use a native <details> and <summary> element when it provides the required behavior.

WCAG requirements

  • The disclosure control has a programmatically determinable accessible name that identifies the content or action it controls. (2.4.6, 4.1.2)
  • The disclosure control exposes button semantics. A native <summary> within <details> provides the native disclosure interaction; a custom disclosure should use a native <button> where feasible. (4.1.2)
  • A custom disclosure button has aria-expanded="false" when the associated content is hidden and aria-expanded="true" when the associated content is visible. (4.1.2)
  • A custom disclosure button identifies its controlled content with aria-controls set to the id of the controlled section. (1.3.1, 4.1.2)
  • The disclosure control is operable using a keyboard. (2.1.1)
  • A native button or summary responds to its native keyboard behavior. A custom button responds to Enter and Space without causing the page to scroll unexpectedly. (2.1.1)
  • The disclosure control has a visible focus indicator when it receives keyboard focus. (2.4.7)
  • The control and newly revealed content appear in a meaningful reading and focus order. (1.3.2, 2.4.3)
  • When content is hidden, controls within that content do not receive keyboard focus or remain available as active controls. (2.1.1, 2.4.3)
  • When content is revealed, its controls and content become available through normal reading and keyboard navigation. (1.3.1, 2.1.1)
  • Expanding or collapsing the disclosure does not cause an unexpected change of context or move focus unexpectedly. (3.2.1, 3.2.2, 2.4.3)
  • When the expanded or collapsed state of the disclosure trigger is communicated visually, color is not the only visual means used to convey that state. (1.4.1)
  • Visible control text is included in the accessible name. (2.5.3)
  • The disclosure control and any state-indicating icon have sufficient contrast. (1.4.3, 1.4.11)
  • The disclosure control remains usable at 200% text resize, at a 320-CSS-pixel-wide viewport, and when text-spacing overrides are applied. (1.4.4, 1.4.10, 1.4.12)
  • The disclosure control meets the minimum target-size requirement or an applicable exception applies. (2.5.8)
  • Focused disclosure controls are not completely hidden by sticky headers, banners, dialogs, or other author-created content. (2.4.11)
  • If the disclosure is a navigation menu, the option corresponding to the current page is indicated with aria-current="page". (4.1.2)

Best practices

These practices improve usability and robustness, although not all are separate WCAG requirements in every implementation.

  • Prefer native <details> and <summary> when the native interaction and presentation meet the design requirements.
  • Use a native <button> for a custom disclosure trigger rather than recreating button behavior with a <div>, <span>, or link.
  • Use a concise label that identifies the related content or describes the result of activation, such as Show shipping details or More options.
  • Keep the control label stable where possible. Communicate expanded and collapsed state separately rather than changing the name from Show details to Hide details.
  • Provide a visual state indicator, such as a chevron, plus/minus icon, or label change. Do not rely on the icon alone.
  • Keep the disclosure control adjacent to, and before, the content it shows or hides.
  • Make the full visible control area clickable, not only a small icon or text fragment.
  • Do not automatically expand a disclosure when it receives keyboard focus.
  • Do not automatically collapse a disclosure merely because focus leaves it, unless the interaction is a clearly identified and appropriate menu or popover pattern.
  • Do not move focus into the revealed content automatically unless the interaction requires it, such as opening a navigation menu in a specific supported design pattern.
  • Do not use a disclosure pattern when the hidden content is essential to understanding or completing the primary task.
  • For content that users may need to print, consider expanding disclosures in print styles or otherwise ensuring the content is available in the printed output.
  • Do not use aria-hidden="true" on a container that contains keyboard-focusable descendants unless those descendants are also made unfocusable.
  • Do not use aria-expanded to describe a checkbox, toggle button, menu button, tab, tree item, or other control whose semantics are better represented by a different pattern.

Practical test sequence

Focus Details Applicable Criteria
Identify function
  • Confirm that the component is a disclosure rather than an accordion, dialog, menu button, tab, checkbox, or another more appropriate pattern. (1.3.1, 4.1.2)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 1.3.1 Info and Relationships (Level A)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 4.1.2 Name, Role, Value (Level A)
Semantics and language
  • Confirm that a native <details>/<summary> implementation is used where feasible, or that a custom disclosure trigger is a native <button> or exposes button semantics. (4.1.2)
  • Confirm that the visible label describes the topic of the controlled content or the action performed. (2.4.6)
  • Confirm that the visible label is included in the accessible name. (2.5.3)
  • For a custom disclosure, confirm that aria-expanded matches the visual state and that aria-controls, when used, references the correct content element. (1.3.1, 4.1.2)
  • Confirm that the control appears before the related content in the reading order. (1.3.2)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 1.3.1 Info and Relationships (Level A)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 1.3.2 Meaningful Sequence (Level A)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 2.4.6 Headings and Labels (Level AA)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 2.5.3 Label in Name (Level A)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 4.1.2 Name, Role, Value (Level A)
Screen reader
  • Confirm that the trigger is announced as a button or as a native disclosure summary, with a meaningful accessible name. (4.1.2)
  • Confirm that decorative chevrons, plus/minus icons, and other non-text state indicators are not announced unnecessarily. (1.1.1)
  • For a custom disclosure, confirm that the collapsed or expanded state is announced when the control receives focus. (4.1.2)
  • Expand and collapse the disclosure and confirm that the state updates correctly. (4.1.2)
  • Confirm that the revealed content is available in a meaningful reading order after the trigger. (1.3.2)
  • When collapsed, confirm that hidden content is not presented as available content and does not contain reachable controls. (1.3.1, 4.1.2)
  • When expanded, confirm that links, form controls, and other interactive content in the revealed section are available. (1.3.1, 2.1.1)
  • For navigation menus, confirm the current page option is identified as such. (4.1.2)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 1.1.1 Non-text Content (Level A)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 1.3.1 Info and Relationships (Level A)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 1.3.2 Meaningful Sequence (Level A)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 2.1.1 Keyboard (Level A)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 4.1.2 Name, Role, Value (Level A)
Keyboard only
  • Use Tab and Shift+Tab to reach and move away from the disclosure control. (2.1.1, 2.4.3)
  • Use Enter and Space to expand and collapse the disclosure. Keyboard activation should perform the same function as pointer activation, and Space should not scroll the page unexpectedly. (2.1.1)
  • Confirm that the disclosure control has a visible focus indicator and is not completely hidden by other author-created content. (2.4.7, 2.4.11)
  • Confirm that focus remains on the disclosure control after it is expanded or collapsed unless the interaction pattern requires another logical destination. (2.4.3)
  • When the disclosure is expanded, use Tab to confirm that interactive content within the revealed section becomes reachable in a logical order. (2.1.1, 2.4.3)
  • When the disclosure is collapsed, confirm that its hidden interactive content does not receive keyboard focus. (2.1.1, 2.4.3)
  • Confirm that focus does not become trapped in the revealed content. (2.1.2)
  • Confirm that the disclosure does not automatically expand when it receives focus. (3.2.1)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 2.1.1 Keyboard (Level A)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 2.1.2 No Keyboard Trap (Level A)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 2.4.3 Focus Order (Level A)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 2.4.7 Focus Visible (Level AA)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (Minimum) (Level AA)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 3.2.1 On Focus (Level A)
Inspect visual aspects
  • Check contrast for the disclosure label, button boundary where required, state indicator, and focus indicator. (1.4.3, 1.4.11)
  • Confirm that expanded and collapsed states are not distinguished by color alone. (1.4.1)
  • Check 200% text resize, a 320-CSS-pixel-wide viewport, and text-spacing overrides. The disclosure label, state indicator, and revealed content must remain visible, usable, and unclipped. (1.4.4, 1.4.10, 1.4.12)
  • Confirm that the disclosure trigger meets the minimum target size or has sufficient spacing from adjacent targets. (2.5.8)
  • Confirm that expanding the content does not leave the trigger or newly revealed content in an unusable position, such as hidden behind a sticky element or outside the visible area without a predictable scroll position. (1.4.10, 2.4.11)
