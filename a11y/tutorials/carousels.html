Carousels

A carousel is a set of related items that can be viewed by moving between slides or by scrolling through a horizontal collection. A carousel may display one slide at a time or several items at once.

Carousels may include previous and next buttons, slide-picker controls, scroll buttons, automatic rotation, or touch and pointer gestures. A carousel that displays several cards in a horizontal row may also be described as a horizontal scroller or multi-item carousel.

WCAG requirements

  • The carousel has a programmatically determinable name when its purpose or boundaries are not otherwise clear. (1.3.1, 2.4.6, 4.1.2)
  • Previous, next, pause, play, and slide-picker controls have accessible names that describe their purpose. (2.4.6, 4.1.2)
  • Carousel controls are operable with a keyboard. (2.1.1)
  • Carousel controls have a visible focus indicator. (2.4.7)
  • The current slide or item is programmatically identified when the carousel provides a selected or current state. (1.3.1, 4.1.2)
  • Slide-picker controls expose which slide is currently selected, where slide-picker controls are provided. (4.1.2)
  • Content that is visually hidden because it is outside the current view remains available through an accessible interaction, unless it is intentionally inactive or unavailable. (2.1.1, 4.1.2)
  • If the carousel automatically rotates, users can pause or stop the rotation. (2.2.2)
  • When a carousel automatically rotates, the rotation stops when a user focuses within the carousel or hovers over it. (2.2.2)
  • Carousel movement does not prevent users from reading or operating the content. (2.2.2)
  • Changing slides does not cause an unexpected change of context or move focus unexpectedly. (3.2.1, 3.2.2, 2.4.3)
  • Text alternatives are provided for meaningful images within slides. (1.1.1)
  • Slide content and controls appear in a meaningful reading and focus order. (1.3.2, 2.4.3)
  • Visible labels for carousel controls are included in their accessible names. (2.5.3)
  • The carousel remains usable when text is enlarged, spacing is increased, or the viewport is narrow. (1.4.4, 1.4.10, 1.4.12)
  • Carousel controls and important visual indicators have sufficient contrast. (1.4.3, 1.4.11)
  • Carousel state is not conveyed by color alone. (1.4.1)
  • Carousel controls meet the minimum target-size requirement or an applicable exception applies. (2.5.8)
  • Focused carousel controls are not completely hidden by other author-created content. (2.4.11)

Best practices

These practices improve usability and robustness, although not all are separate WCAG requirements in every implementation.

  • Use a native <button> for previous, next, pause, play, and slide-picker controls whenever possible.
  • Give the carousel a visible heading when it represents a distinct section of the page.
  • Use specific control names such as Next slide and Previous slide rather than ambiguous names such as Right and Left.
  • Use an accessible name for icon-only controls, and hide decorative arrow icons from assistive technology.
  • Do not automatically rotate content unless there is a strong reason to do so.
  • Provide a clearly visible pause or stop button for an automatically rotating carousel.
  • Stop automatic rotation when the carousel receives keyboard focus or pointer hover.
  • Do not restart automatic rotation unexpectedly after a user pauses it.
  • Keep focus on the activated control when moving between slides unless the interaction requires a different focus destination.
  • Do not move focus to a slide merely because the carousel changes visually.
  • Ensure that users can reach all slides or cards without relying on a mouse, touch gesture, or drag.
  • For a horizontal card carousel, provide usable scroll buttons when native scrolling alone is not sufficient for discovering additional content.
  • Do not hide cards from the accessibility tree only because they are outside the visible portion of a horizontal scroller.
  • Use a list structure for a collection of peer cards when that structure accurately represents the content.
  • Do not use role="slider" for a carousel. The ARIA slider role is for selecting a value within a range.
  • Make the entire visible control target large enough to activate, not just a small arrow or icon.
  • Ensure that slide transitions do not cause content to flash, move rapidly, or create a seizure or motion concern.
  • Provide meaningful alternative text for informative images and empty alternative text for decorative images.
  • Ensure that each slide has a meaningful heading or accessible identification when users need to distinguish slides.
  • Do not use slide-picker dots as the only way to determine how many slides are available or which slide is current.

Practical test sequence

Focus Details Applicable criteria
Semantics and language
  • Confirm the carousel has an appropriate visible heading or accessible name when its purpose is not otherwise clear. (1.3.1, 2.4.6, 4.1.2)
  • Confirm previous, next, pause, play, and slide-picker controls are native <button> elements where feasible, or expose the correct button semantics. (4.1.2)
  • Confirm each control has a meaningful name that describes its action. (2.4.6, 4.1.2)
  • Confirm the carousel's slides or cards have an appropriate structure and appear in a meaningful order. (1.3.1, 1.3.2)
  • For a horizontal card carousel, confirm that the cards are represented as a coherent collection, such as a list when appropriate. (1.3.1)
  • If similar carousels appear across multiple pages, confirm that they are identified consistently. (3.2.4)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 1.3.1 Info and Relationships (Level A)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 1.3.2 Meaningful Sequence (Level A)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 2.4.6 Headings and Labels (Level AA)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 3.2.4 Consistent Identification (Level AA)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 4.1.2 Name, Role, Value (Level A)
Screen reader
  • Confirm the carousel is identified by an appropriate name when necessary. (1.3.1, 4.1.2)
  • Verify that previous, next, pause, play, and slide-picker controls are announced with meaningful names and as buttons. (4.1.2)
  • Verify that visible control text is included in the accessible name. Decorative icons should not be announced. (1.1.1, 2.5.3, 4.1.2)
  • Confirm the current slide or selected slide-picker control is identified programmatically. (4.1.2)
  • When a slide changes, confirm that users can determine which slide or item is now available without an unexpected focus change. (4.1.2, 3.2.2)
  • Confirm that all available slides or cards can be reached and read, including content outside the initial visual viewport. (1.3.1, 2.1.1)
  • For a horizontal card carousel, confirm that cards are not incorrectly exposed as hidden merely because they are outside the visible scroll area. (1.3.1, 4.1.2)
  • Confirm that meaningful images have appropriate alternative text. (1.1.1)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 1.1.1 Non-text Content (Level A)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 1.3.1 Info and Relationships (Level A)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 2.1.1 Keyboard (Level A)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 2.5.3 Label in Name (Level A)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 4.1.2 Name, Role, Value (Level A)
Keyboard only
  • Use Tab and Shift+Tab to reach every carousel control. (2.1.1)
  • Use Enter and Space to activate previous, next, pause, play, and slide-picker buttons. Keyboard activation should perform the same function as a mouse click. (2.1.1)
  • Confirm that the page does not scroll unexpectedly when Space activates a button. (2.1.1)
  • Confirm that every focused control has a visible focus indicator and is not completely hidden by other content. (2.4.7, 2.4.11)
  • Confirm that controls and slide content appear in a logical focus order. (2.4.3)
  • Confirm that users can reach every slide or card without using a pointer, touch gesture, or drag operation. (2.1.1)
  • For an automatically rotating carousel, confirm that rotation stops when focus enters the carousel. (2.2.2)
  • Confirm that slide changes do not unexpectedly move focus to another control or location. (2.4.3, 3.2.2)
  • Confirm that the carousel does not create a keyboard trap. (2.1.2)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 2.1.1 Keyboard (Level A)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 2.1.2 No Keyboard Trap (Level A)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 2.4.3 Focus Order (Level A)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 2.4.7 Focus Visible (Level AA)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (Minimum) (Level AA)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide (Level A)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 3.2.2 On Input (Level A)
Automatic rotation and movement
  • Determine whether the carousel rotates automatically without user activation. (2.2.2)
  • If it rotates automatically, confirm that a visible pause or stop control is available before or while the content moves. (2.2.2)
  • Confirm that the pause or stop control has an accessible name that reflects its current action. (4.1.2)
  • Confirm that rotation stops when the carousel receives keyboard focus. (2.2.2)
  • Confirm that rotation stops while the pointer is over the carousel. (2.2.2)
  • Confirm that the carousel does not restart rotation unexpectedly after the user pauses it. (2.2.2)
  • Verify that moving content does not prevent users from reading, activating controls, or reaching links within the carousel. (2.2.2)
  • Check for excessive flashing or rapid movement. (2.3.1, 2.3.3)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide (Level A)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 2.3.1 Three Flashes or Below Threshold (Level A)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 2.3.3 Animation from Interactions (Level AAA)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 4.1.2 Name, Role, Value (Level A)
Inspect visual aspects
  • Check contrast for slide text, controls, icons, indicators, and focus indicators in all states. (1.4.3, 1.4.11)
  • Confirm that the current slide or selected state is not conveyed by color alone. (1.4.1)
  • Confirm that slide-picker controls have a visible distinction between current and non-current states. (1.4.11)
  • Check 200% text resize, a 320-CSS-pixel-wide viewport, and text-spacing overrides. The carousel, controls, and slide content must remain visible, usable, and unclipped. (1.4.4, 1.4.10, 1.4.12)
  • For a horizontal card carousel, confirm that cards do not become inaccessible because of clipping or unexpected two-dimensional scrolling. (1.4.10)
  • Confirm that controls meet the minimum target size or have sufficient spacing from adjacent targets. (2.5.8)
  • Confirm that focused controls are not obscured by sticky headers, banners, or other author-created content. (2.4.11)
  • Confirm that meaningful images have sufficient text alternatives and that decorative images are not announced. (1.1.1)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 1.1.1 Non-text Content (Level A)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 1.4.1 Use of Color (Level A)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum) (Level AA)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 1.4.4 Resize Text (Level AA)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 1.4.10 Reflow (Level AA)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 1.4.11 Non-text Contrast (Level AA)
  • WCAG 2.2: Understanding SC 1.4.12 Text Spacing (Level AA)
